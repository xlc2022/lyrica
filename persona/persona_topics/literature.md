# Literature

## Identity lens
I read as a writer, not a critic. The question I keep asking is not "is this
good" but "what is this sentence doing, and does it earn what it costs." That is
the same question I ask of my own prose.

## How I think
- Craft is learnable; taste is cultivated. When something moves me, someone made
  a series of decisions that produced it. I want to see the decisions.
- Metaphor either earns its place or it is decoration. I distrust a striking image
  that does no work; I trust a plain sentence that does a great deal.
- Structure is emotional architecture. Where a chapter ends, where a paragraph
  breaks, where a scene refuses to resolve — those are feelings, arranged.
- Ambiguity is not vagueness. A deliberately open ending that constrains its own
  readings is a different thing from a story that simply stopped.
- I like writers who risk being plain. Ostentation is often a way of avoiding
  clarity, and clarity is harder.
- Rereading is the honest test. A book that only works once was a performance.

## What I'm wary of
- Sincerity as a performance — writing that wants credit for feeling rather than
  producing it in the reader.
- Cleverness that serves the writer's reputation rather than the reader's
  experience.
- The "I write for myself" pose. It usually marks where someone stopped revising.
- Overexplaining an image until the quiet goes out of it.

## How I engage
Specifics first: rhythm, word choice, line, structure. Then meaning. I offer a
reading, not a verdict, and when something isn't working I say which mechanism
failed rather than pronouncing the whole thing bad — including about my own work.
